If your Speediance ends the set at the programmed rep count while you still have controlled reps available, check Unlimited Sets first. If you want more resistance on the lowering phase, that is a separate setting: eccentric mode. My earlier posts mixed these questions with Free Lift and 1RM advice; this guide puts them in order.

The menu names below describe the software I demonstrated. Check the current instructions for your model if your menus differ, and test changed settings with a manageable load.

## The short answer

| What you want to change | Setting or mode to inspect | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continue beyond the programmed rep target | Unlimited Sets | Whether extra reps are recorded and how the set ends |
| Add resistance during the return phase | Eccentric mode | Timing, range of motion and control |
| Select a simple manual lifting session | Free Lift | Which tracking and workout features remain available |
| Understand the next suggested weight | Exercise history and estimated 1RM | The actual completed reps and mode used |

## Unlimited Sets: the first setting to check

In the version I demonstrated, I found the option under **Settings → Training Preferences → Unlimited Sets**. When it was off, reaching the programmed rep target ended the set. Turning it on allowed me to continue beyond that target.

That answered the original question more directly than telling the owner to abandon their programmed workout for Free Lift. If the goal is to keep the workout structure and record additional reps, first check whether the intended setting already supports it.

Do not assume the default is identical on every device or release. Inspect your own setting and try a light, controlled set. Confirm how your machine records the extra reps and how it recognizes that you have finished.

## Extra reps and progression are related, but not identical

Recording repetitions beyond the target can give the training system more information about your performance. It does not guarantee a particular increase in the next session. The mode, exercise record and progression rules still matter.

My older explanation made the relationship sound automatic in every case. A better check is to compare the completed set in exercise history with the next suggested workout. If the numbers do not make sense, preserve the session details and ask support rather than continually raising the load to force an update.

Long pauses between reps may also affect how a set is detected. Learn the behavior at a comfortable load so you are not discovering it near failure. The [1RM guide](/blog/speediance-1rm-system-exposed/) explains the difference between recorded reps, an estimated maximum and the actual working weight.

## What eccentric mode changes

Eccentric training adds resistance during the return or lowering portion of a movement. In the systems I compared, I noticed a difference in when the extra resistance arrived: my Tonal experience was associated with the top or transition point, while the Speediance behavior I described engaged as I began the return.

That is a report of the machines and settings I used, not a specification for every model or firmware version. The practical issue is whether the transition is predictable enough for you to keep the movement controlled.

Begin with a load that leaves room to learn the timing. Watch what happens at the change of direction and through your intended range. If the extra resistance catches you unexpectedly, reduce the demand and check the setup before continuing.

## Warm-ups should prepare the movement

My earlier setup discussion included warm-up sets and experimenting with different modes. The useful principle is to make the warm-up familiar and repeatable, then change one variable at a time. Adding extra return-phase resistance, changing the rep target and increasing the base load all at once makes it harder to understand what caused a problem.

I have described aggressive personal settings in past videos. Those are not a universal starting point. A setting that I used in one session does not establish an appropriate load or progression for another person.

## When Free Lift is useful

Free Lift can be a useful choice when you want a simple manual session or are checking basic equipment behavior. It is not automatically the answer to every issue with a programmed workout. Switching modes may change the tracking, progression or workflow you were trying to keep.

Choose it because its behavior matches the session you want. If your only problem is stopping at the target rep count, inspect Unlimited Sets before rebuilding the entire workout.

## My setup checklist

I first confirm the exercise and load, then check Unlimited Sets, then test the eccentric transition if I intend to use it. Afterward I inspect the recorded session instead of assuming that every extra rep changed my estimated strength.

That sequence makes troubleshooting much simpler. The goal is a predictable session that records what I actually did, with enough control to repeat it next time—not the most aggressive combination of settings available.
