## The short answer

**WHOOP 5 is narrower than WHOOP 4, but my hands-on comparison found it thicker off the wrist.** That is the dimension I notice under sleeves and against the wrist bone. The longer battery life is useful, but WHOOP 4 bands and the old charger do not carry forward.

For an existing WHOOP 4 owner, I would upgrade only when the battery improvement and current hardware ecosystem are worth replacing the accessories you already own. If recovery and strain are the features you value and your WHOOP 4 still works, WHOOP 5 is not an automatic upgrade.

## What "smaller" misses

Most reviews repeat a single claim: WHOOP 5 is smaller. That description is incomplete because a wearable has more than one meaningful dimension.

WHOOP made the device narrower. In my direct comparison, though, it sits thicker off the wrist. Width looks good on a specification graphic; thickness is what catches a sleeve and changes how the sensor feels around the wrist bone.

That does not make WHOOP 5 unusable. My point is narrower is not automatically the same as more comfortable, and buyers should not assume the new device disappears more easily on the wrist.

## Bands and charger compatibility

The accessory break is the most frustrating part of the upgrade. WHOOP 4 bands do not fit WHOOP 5, and the WHOOP 4 charger does not carry forward either.

That changes the real upgrade price. Existing owners may already have spare bands for washing and different body-wear options for training. Replacing a working device also means replacing that useful accessory collection.

Before upgrading, price the complete setup you actually use rather than comparing only the device or membership offer.

## Battery is the strongest argument for WHOOP 5

WHOOP 5 has better battery life. That is a real improvement because the value of a recovery wearable depends on wearing it consistently, especially overnight.

Longer time between charges reduces the chance of losing a night of HRV, resting heart rate, sleep, and respiratory-rate data. If charging friction regularly leaves gaps in your WHOOP 4 history, WHOOP 5 solves a practical problem.

If your charging routine already works, the improvement may be convenient rather than decisive.

## Who should upgrade?

### Choose WHOOP 5 if:

- Longer battery life would materially improve data continuity.
- You are buying into WHOOP for the first time.
- You want the current hardware and accessory ecosystem.
- Replacing bands and charging hardware does not erase the upgrade value.

### Keep WHOOP 4 if:

- Your current sensor, bands, and charger still work reliably.
- The recovery and strain workflow is the main reason you use WHOOP.
- A thicker off-wrist profile would be a comfort concern.
- You would rather wait for a more meaningful sensor or workflow improvement.

## Where WHOOP fits in my tracker stack

I use WHOOP to understand recovery response: HRV, resting heart rate, sleep, respiratory rate, and strain. I use Garmin to record the workout itself, especially GPS, pace, distance, and heart-rate zones.

That distinction matters more than treating every wearable as a direct replacement for every other wearable. You can see my current measurements on the [live WHOOP data page](/whoop/) or use the [fitness tracker comparison](/compare-trackers/) to choose by training goal.

## Bottom line

WHOOP 5 is a narrower device with better battery life, but in my hands-on comparison it is not simply smaller in every way that affects comfort. It is thicker off the wrist, and the accessory transition makes the total upgrade more expensive than the device comparison alone suggests.

For a new WHOOP buyer, WHOOP 5 is the current choice. For a satisfied WHOOP 4 owner, upgrade when battery and current hardware solve a real problem, not because a marketing line says the new device is smaller.
